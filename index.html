A landmark saved for Beach Haven

Before it became your inn by the sea, this was the grand summer home of Dr. Edward H. Williams — and a piece of Beach Haven nearly lost.

Dr. Edward H. Williams’ retreat

In 1888, Dr. Edward H. Williams of Philadelphia commissioned this remarkable Queen Anne home as his summer escape at the shore. Wilson Brothers & Company designed it for drama: a sweeping porch, generous rooms, and an unforgettable silhouette against the sky.

Learn about the Williams Cottage Inn

The house Beach Haven remembers

Its onion-shaped cupola gave the home its beloved local name: “the Onion Dome.” For generations, it has been one of the island’s most recognizable Victorian landmarks.

Beach Haven Historic District

Saved from the wrecking ball

After years of decline, the house stood on the edge of demolition. The Blahut family stepped in to save it, undertaking a painstaking three-year restoration so its next chapter could be shared rather than erased.

National Register record

Its story continues with you

Today, original character lives alongside the comforts of a stay by the sea: period antiques, artwork, gracious gathering rooms, and eight distinctive guest rooms. Thank you for becoming part of the story.
